## Projects (step 3)

Also pre-select any project that is a repo in the `ai-foundry-byu` GitHub organization, or is
listed under **Where the work lives** in the README. Client repos are the usual place Foundry work
happens. Summarize their commits by date, message, and file name only, as `SKILL.md` already
requires: client code and data never go in the report.

## The report (step 6)

The plan has a **Menu** line with one or two items. Write **Against your plan** item by item:

```markdown
## Against your plan

**Goal was:** <from the plan file>

**<menu item 1, as written in the plan>:** <does the work match it? Say it plainly either way.>

**<menu item 2>:** <same>
```

For a **Learn** item, also say whether the proof named in Done looks like is present: the
certificate (a link or a file in the repo) and the deliverable, or the `/learn` logs in the
window and a practice exam result. State what you found; do not judge it.

## The retro (step 7)

Say "Six quick questions for your retro" and ask these six in place of the five in `SKILL.md`:

1. **Did you hit the goal?** Yes, partly, or no, and why.
2. **What did the client or your users say or do about the work?** For a Learn-only sprint: who
   did you show it to, and what did they say?
3. **What did the report show you** that you didn't expect, or wouldn't have noticed yourself?
4. **If the plan changed, why?** (Skip it if the plan did not change.)
5. **Actual difficulty, 1 to 5,** and why it differed from the difficulty you predicted.
6. **What will you change next sprint?** One specific thing.

The **Your retro** section gets one line per question in the same order, with question 2 written
as **What did the client or your users say?**. People are named by role ("the client contact",
"a user"), never by name.
